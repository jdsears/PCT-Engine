# How this market buys

The clearest statement of the buying structure the project has had, from two
sources in one week of August 2026: a live reply from VIRTUS and James's note
that followed it. Recorded so future briefs start from it rather than
rediscover it. None of the commercial detail below ever appears in outbound
wording; it shapes who the engine targets and how hard, never what an email
says.

## The structure, from a live operator reply

Pete Betts, Fellow of Data Centre Design at VIRTUS, 7 August 2026, replying
to Patrick's cold open:

> These types of products are only performance spec'd by us and our
> consultants. We build all our DCs using a General Contractor and their
> sub-contractors.

The operator and their design consultants write the performance spec. A
general contractor and its subcontractors do the buying. Three parties, three
conversations: operators and consultants about specification, contractors
about supply.

## The commercial consequences, James's note verbatim

> The sales cycle for these types of projects can be very strange.
>
> If PCT get onto the design of a DC via the operator or consultant we can
> receive a commission from Richards just for that. Even if ultimitly they do
> not purchase through us, we would be rewarded for getting on the design.
> Note that this often happens outside the UK.
> For example we picked up an RFQ from Ireland last week for a DC being built
> in the north east, engineering work was all done in Ireland but now they
> are in the procurement stage they are looking for UK suppliers.
> On the flip side lots of UK engineering houses often do design /
> engineering work for the middle east for Oil and Gas for example.
>
> Often also once the design work is all done and then when it goes out to
> the GC they will go to market to find best prices for items on the design.
> We can also pickup this work if we are bidding in the UK only to a UK
> customer because of our distribution agreement.

## The commission structure, six packages

James, 7 August 2026: Richards splits a project's commission across six
packages, each earned by the representative who did that part of the work.
The percentage split between them lives with John and James, deliberately
not in this repository. The structure, compressed from the definitions
James shared:

1. **The inquiry.** Introducing the products on a sales call, adoption onto
   a project's approved vendor list, considered as basis of design;
   "essentially ours to lose at this stage". Not merely having a contact at
   the customer.
2. **The quotation.** Preparing the original technical and commercial quote,
   assisting with or completing sizing and selection. Not just pricing a
   fully specified list.
3. **Revision.** Technical revision of an existing quote, a part number or
   model change. Not letterhead or quantity changes.
4. **Negotiation.** Terms, conditions, placement.
5. **Invoicing in the contractual territory.** Order processing, follow-up
   and modification; the location initiating order placement, not an HQ
   bill-to address.
6. **The final destination.** After-sales in the country where the plant
   runs: warranty, training, spare parts service.

## Where the engine's work lands on the packages

- **Package 1 is the outbound-to-spec play.** The consultant campaign, the
  operator briefing that aims at the specification decision, and the
  design-in outcome are all package 1 capture: onto the approved vendor
  list, basis of design, ours to lose.
- **Package 2 is the parked quoting engine.** The largest single share of
  the split, per James, and it is sizing, selection and original quotation,
  which is exactly what the configurator and the price machinery already
  half-know how to do. When John calls the quoting engine build, this is
  the commercial case for it.
- **Packages 3 to 5 land in the UK even when the design sat abroad.** The
  Ireland RFQ is the live proof: engineering done in Dublin, procurement
  seeking UK suppliers, so the quoting and invoicing packages arrive at the
  UK rep. The gate already keeps these stories, because a UK build is a UK
  story wherever it was designed.
- **Package 6 defaults to the UK rep on UK builds**, which is after-sales
  the engine never touches but the reps collect.
- **The parked foreign-design signal now has a precise value statement**: a
  UK or Irish house designing a foreign plant is package 1 and 2 territory
  even when the build never touches the UK. Still parked for a calibrated
  gate change, now with its worth written down.

## What the engine takes from it

1. **Two prizes, not one.** A UK purchase, and specified-on-design, which
   carries a commission from Richards in its own right. The pipeline records
   design-in as its own outcome (migration 029); it qualifies a lead the way
   a meeting does.
2. **The consultant is a first-class target.** Company type consultant on the
   DC campaign, seeded curated, drafted to about specification fit and
   approved equals, never supply or price.
3. **Design geography is wider than build geography.** The spec for a UK
   build can sit in Dublin (the Ireland RFQ), and UK houses design foreign
   plants. Hence the Irish design-house carve-out to the prospecting
   geography rule, and, parked for a calibrated gate change, the phase-two
   idea that a foreign project keeps value when its design house is in scope.
4. **Supply bids stay UK to UK customers**, per the distribution agreement,
   which the campaign gates already respect.
